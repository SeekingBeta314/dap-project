# Training the depth student

The student runs at 20 Hz. It receives the three latest delayed 212 × 120 depth frames and the robot's 31 proprioceptive values. It holds still until a frame arrives. Each 2 × 2 image block contributes its nearest valid depth. All three half-resolution frames enter a convolutional encoder with four stride-two layers (16, 24, 32 and 40 channels). Its spatial features and normalized proprioception feed a 192- and 128-unit network that estimates the partner palm's position, velocity, normal and finger axis at the newest frame's capture time. The axes are orthogonalized. A frozen copy of the selected teacher's actor turns the estimate and the robot's current state into seven bounded joint velocities. The deployed checkpoint contains both networks.

The simulator supplies the true partner pose and velocity at the capture time for offline supervision. For action distillation, the same frozen actor runs twice on each training sample. One pass receives the inferred partner state and the other receives the training label. We minimize the mean squared difference between these actions plus a smooth L1 error on the standardized partner state. Pose-only warm-up uses the second term. The final closed-loop scores use the deployed student's estimates throughout.

The encoder and pose head contain 270,636 trainable parameters. The actor contains 81,927 frozen parameters. We kept spatial features to preserve image location and used a supervised pose loss to give the camera network a training signal even when different partner poses yield the same clipped action. During fitting we shift the entire frame stack together by up to one pooled pixel and hide the normalized proprioceptive input to the pose head for 35% of samples. The actor always receives the robot's actual arm state. This reduces the incentive to infer the partner's position solely from the robot's own wrist on successful demonstrations. The architecture, augmentation magnitude, proprioception masking rate, optimization settings and rollout counts are project design choices.

An episode uses only the nominal training RNG stream or the nominal training bank. Collection stores the delivered image stack and the matched privileged vector for offline labeling. Closed-loop validation uses an independent validation stream. Checkpoint selection maximizes aligned first contacts on nominal validation seeds 300–363, with ties resolved by lower mean benchmark loss. The held-out evaluation stream is separate.

The planned selection candidates use the same selected teacher: a fit on the initial demonstration corpus, a fit after adding trajectories visited by an early student, and a fit after a further round driven by the updated student. The initial data combine privileged servo demonstrations and trajectories from the earlier IK-goal policy, which expose the vision network to arm configurations away from expert demonstrations. A provisional student with the teacher's warm-start actor collects the first learner trajectories. Its validation check is diagnostic. The selected teacher labels all final candidate training sets.

## Follow-up to the initial validation failure

The provisional student achieved 2 aligned contacts on the 64 selection scenarios. In 48 of its own training-stream rollouts, the median estimated position error was about 6–7 cm and mid-reach finger-axis error exceeded 110° at the 80th percentile. This motivated a second architecture that learns an image heatmap for the palm, uses camera calibration to convert the estimated image location and depth into a world position, and reads an image patch centered on the palm. It learns the finger axis and its sign separately. All camera observations, teacher labels and evaluation rules are shared with the first architecture.

The follow-up has two candidates: a fit using the initial demonstration and learner-trajectory sets and a fit after visiting further training scenarios with this spatial model. We choose within each architecture on nominal validation seeds 300–363. The winners are compared on the same fresh nominal validation seeds 400–463. We freeze the model with more aligned contacts there, resolving ties by lower mean benchmark loss. These two cohorts serve model selection and remain separate from the final evaluation.
