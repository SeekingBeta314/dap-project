## Q1. Teacher-student / privileged learning landmarks: what was privileged, how the student was trained, the student's observations, sample counts, pitfalls

### Takeaway
The standard recipe across driving, legged locomotion and dexterous manipulation has three steps. First, train a teacher with RL (or IL) on compact privileged state. Second, distill it into a sensor-based student with on-policy DAgger-style supervision: the student drives the rollouts and the teacher labels them. Third, add memory (GRU, TCN or history) so the student can infer what the teacher saw directly. Reported student-phase budgets are small next to teacher RL: about 6M samples (Agarwal), about 80M (RMA) and about 1 day (Visual Dexterity stage 2), against 1.2B–15B for the teachers. The main failure mode is teacher information the student cannot infer from its observation history.

### Cited Findings
**Learning by Cheating (Chen et al., CoRL 2019) — driving, RGB camera**
- Privileged agent: a ground-truth bird's-eye map "M ∈ {0,1}^(W×H×7)" holding lanes, traffic-light state, vehicles and pedestrians, at "resolution 320×320" covering "a 64m×64m square". It was trained by behavior cloning from autopilot trajectories, with random rotation and shift augmentation of the map — [arXiv 1912.12294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)
- Student training: first "offline behavior cloning on all command-conditioned branches" on the stage-1 states, then on-policy training "via DAgger, using the privileged agent as an oracle". The authors note that "online training with DAgger is slower than training on pre-existing trajectories, so we use the first stage to accelerate" — [arXiv 1912.12294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)
- "White-box" supervision: the teacher gives an action for every possible navigation command in every visited state, so "all conditional branches of the privileged agent can train all branches of the sensorimotor agent in parallel". The authors call this "a powerful form of data augmentation" — [arXiv 1912.12294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)
- Data: "100 training trajectories at 10 fps, which amount to about 157K frames … and 4 hours of driving". Teacher backbone was ResNet-18 from scratch; student was an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-34 on a single forward-facing camera, with CIL image augmentations (pixel dropout, blur, Gaussian noise, color perturbation) — [arXiv 1912.12294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)
- Ablation (Table 1, CoRL2017 navigation, test town and weather): "Direct" one-stage imitation of the autopilot reached 20% success. The two-stage variants scored 16, 64, 96 and 100, adding white-box and/or on-policy supervision, and both together reached 100%. The PDF text extraction garbled the column alignment, so I could not confirm whether 64 or 96 is white-box-only. The paper's summary: "white-box supervision and on-policy trajectories – each substantially improve performance and together achieve 100% success rate" — [arXiv 1912.12294](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.12294)

**Lee et al. 2020 (Science Robotics) — blind ANYmal locomotion; the "student" uses proprioceptive history**
- Teacher privileged info: "contact states, contact forces, terrain profile, friction coeff." and disturbances. The teacher is an MLP trained by policy-gradient RL on procedurally generated terrains (hills, steps, stairs) with an adaptive particle-filter terrain curriculum — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251)
- Student: a TCN over the last N proprioceptive observations, trained with "the dataset aggregation strategy (DAgger)". The loss is action imitation plus latent matching: L = (ā_t(o_t,x_t) − a_t(o_t,H))² + (l̄_t(o_t,x_t) − l_t(H))². A supplementary ablation trains "TCN-100 naive IL" on the action term alone — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251)
- History length matters: "The deviation of the TCN-100 controller was 35.5% lower than that of TCN-1" on a disturbance test. A GRU student landed "between that of TCN-20 and TCN-100" and "fails to achieve the performance of TCN-100 in step experiments" — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251)
- A TCN-20 trained directly with RL, without privileged training, learns much worse than the distilled TCN-20 (Fig. 4E–G learning curves) — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251)
- Probing showed that terrain and contact information "can be (partially) recovered" from proprioceptive history: a decoder reconstructs privileged state from TCN embeddings — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251)

**Miki et al. 2022 (Science Robotics) — perceptive ANYmal locomotion from noisy height samples**
- Teacher privileged info (Table S3, via ar5iv): contact states, contact forces, contact normals, friction, thigh/shank contacts, external forces and torques, airtime, plus *noiseless* terrain samples. Trained with PPO in RaiSim using 1000 parallel environments and 250 steps per environment per iteration at 50 Hz — [ar5iv 2201.08117](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.08117)
- Student: rolls out its own policy (DAgger-style) with loss L_bc + 0.5·L_reconstruction, where reconstruction targets the noiseless height samples and privileged state. Architecture is a "belief encoder" (2-layer GRU, 50 hidden units, truncated BPTT of 10) with an attentional gate that learns how much to trust exteroception versus proprioception. It used 300 environments × 400 steps per iteration and at least about 100 epochs — [ar5iv 2201.08117](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.08117)
- Noise model during student training: per-point lateral and height noise, per-foot offsets, per-episode offsets, random outliers, and cell-based offsets. Episodes were drawn 60% nominal, 30% "offset" (pose drift) and 10% "noisy" (occlusion or mapping failure), with the noise curriculum ramped from epoch 20 to 100 — [ar5iv 2201.08117](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2201.08117)

**RMA (Kumar et al., RSS 2021) — the student regresses the teacher's latent, not its action**
- Privileged e_t (17-D: mass and CoM, motor strengths, friction, local terrain height) is encoded into an 8-D extrinsics vector z_t. The base policy was trained with PPO for 15,000 iterations × 80,000 transitions, about 1.2B steps and about 24 h on "an ordinary desktop with 1 GPU" — [ar5iv 2107.04034](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.04034)
- Adaptation module: 50 steps (0.5 s) of state-action history → MLP → 1-D CNN, trained with MSE to z_t. Data come from rolling out the base policy with the *predicted* ẑ_t (on-policy, DAgger-style), for 1,000 iterations, about 80M steps and about 3 h — [ar5iv 2107.04034](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.04034)
- Sim results (Table II): Expert (privileged) 76.2% success; RMA 73.5%; Robust/DR 62.4%; SysID 56.5%; RMA without adaptation 52.1%. The adaptation module runs at 10 Hz and the base policy at 100 Hz — [ar5iv 2107.04034](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.04034)

**Agarwal et al. 2022 (CoRL) — end-to-end egocentric depth locomotion**
- Phase 1 privileged input: "low resolution scandots located under the robot as a proxy for depth images", plus RMA-style e_t (CoM, friction, motor strength). Trained with PPO and BPTT truncated at 24 steps — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)
- Why not direct RL on depth: "depth rendering slows down the simulation by an order of magnitude, directly training this system using reinforcement learning (RL) would require billions of samples" — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)
- Phase 2: "We train with DAgger … with truncated backpropagation through time (BPTT) to minimize mean squared error between predicted and ground truth actions". The student is ConvNet(depth) → GRU (monolithic), or a version that regresses the teacher's latents (RMA-style) — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)
- Depth input: camera at 480×848 every "100ms ±20ms", cropped, nearest-neighbor hole-filled and "downsampling to 58×87". Depth "has a latency of 10±10ms which we account for during phase 2". "We simulate a latency of 40ms in both the phases of training to match the hardware" is said of the "Noisy" elevation-map baseline (Sec. 3, Baselines), not the main scandot teacher (checked against the ar5iv text, 2026-11-19). The height-map update interval is randomized over [80, 120] ms with [10, 30] ms latency — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)
- Compute: "around 2e5 time-steps per second on a Nvidia RTX 3090 … with 4096 robots", and "We run phase 1 for 15 billion samples (13 hours) and phase 2 for 6 million samples (6 hours)". Both phases fit "on a single GPU in a few days". GRUs are single-layer — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)
- Design for inferability: "we choose scandots and camera field-of-view such that phase 2 loss is low". Thm 2.1 bounds the student's suboptimality by the teacher-student action gap η — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)

**Robot Parkour Learning (Zhuang et al., CoRL 2023)**
- Privileged visual info for each skill teacher: distance to obstacle, obstacle height and width, and a 4-D one-hot obstacle category. Privileged physics info is also given. Teachers are GRU(256) + MLP[512,256,128], trained with PPO, first with soft dynamics constraints (12 h on one RTX 3090) and then hard dynamics (6 h) — [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)
- Distillation: DAgger with a bounded-action divergence loss. The student is CNN (channels [16,32,32], kernels [5,4,3], embedding 128) → GRU(256) → MLP[512,256,128] on 48×64 depth. Distillation ran on 4 machines with one RTX 3090 each — [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)
- Depth sim-to-real: "depth clipping, pixel-level Gaussian noise, and random artifacts" in sim; clipping, hole filling, and spatial and temporal smoothing on the real camera. Depth refreshes at 10 Hz while the policy runs at 50 Hz, asynchronously. Randomized: "depth perception latency, camera position, field of view and proprioception delay" — [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)
- Ablations (Table 2, sim success %, climb/leap/crawl/tilt/run): "No Distill" (direct PPO from depth with a GRU) scored 0/0/73/0/100. "MLP" (no memory) scored 0/1/63/43/100. The distilled GRU policy scored 86/80/100/73/100. Privileged oracles scored 95/82/100/100/100 — [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)

**Extreme Parkour (Cheng et al., 2023)**
- Phase 1 privileged info: scandots, environment parameters, and an oracle heading computed from waypoints. Uses Regularized Online Adaptation (ROA) for the proprioceptive estimator — [arXiv 2309.14341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14341)
- Phase 2: a ConvNet-GRU on depth trained with DAgger on phase-1 actions. "We initialize the actor network with a copy from phase 1 to minimize the drift". Heading cannot be given at deployment, so the student also predicts the oracle heading from depth, and the authors "propose to use a mixture of teacher and student (MTS)" for rollouts — [arXiv 2309.14341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14341)
- Sensor details: D435 at "10 ±2Hz", depth downsampled to 58×87, a constant 0.08 s depth latency and 0.016 s proprioception latency enforced in sim. "The deployable policy can be trained on a single 3090 GPU in less than 20 hours" — [arXiv 2309.14341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14341)

**Visual Dexterity (Chen et al., Science Robotics 2023; precursor CoRL 2021)**
- Teacher privileged state: joint pos/vel, fingertip poses and velocities, object pose and velocity, target quaternion and rotation difference (19G+21 dims, G = number of fingers). Trained with PPO, MLP 512-256-256 — [ar5iv 2211.11744](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.11744)
- Student: one RealSense depth camera → point cloud, merged with a proprioceptive point cloud rendered from the robot's own link meshes via forward kinematics. Goal given as a point cloud. Sparse 3D conv net (Minkowski Engine) + GRU, trained with DAgger in two stages: synthetic unrendered clouds (under 3 days; the paper calls this a 7× speedup over 20+ days for rendered) then rendered and occluded clouds (about 1 day). The summary says total training took under 400 h on one V100 32 GB but also mentions an RTX 3090 for teachers; this is unresolved — [ar5iv 2211.11744](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.11744)
- Inferability: the student has no direct object velocity, and the paper notes "it is necessary to integrate temporal information" (handled by the GRU). Symmetric objects are excluded to avoid multimodal pose targets — [ar5iv 2211.11744](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.11744)
- CoRL 2021 precursor: over 2000 objects, and policies distilled "to use observations easily available in the real world" — [arXiv 2111.03043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.03043)

**OpenAI in-hand manipulation (2018) — asymmetric PPO, vision as a separate estimator**
- Policy inputs: fingertip positions, object position, relative target orientation. Value function additionally gets object orientation and velocities and hand joint angles and velocities (privileged). LSTM policy (1024 dense + 512 LSTM) — [ar5iv 1808.00177](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.00177)
- Vision is a separate CNN pose estimator over 3 RGB cameras at 200×200, trained only on synthetic renders, not end-to-end — [ar5iv 1808.00177](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.00177)
- Compute: 384 machines × 16 CPU cores for rollouts plus 8 V100s. About 100 years of experience (about 50 h) for the fully randomized task — [ar5iv 1808.00177](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.00177)
- Memory matters (Table 5, real robot, consecutive successes): LSTM policy 18.8±17.1 (median 13), feed-forward policy with LSTM value 4.7±4.1, FF/FF 4.6±4.3. The asymmetric value function itself was not ablated — [ar5iv 1808.00177](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.00177)
- The Rubik's cube follow-up adds automatic domain randomization (ADR) and reports "emergent meta-learning" in memory-augmented policies — [arXiv 1910.07113](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07113)

### Inferences
- The closest structural match to dap is Agarwal 2022 / Extreme Parkour: depth CNN + GRU student, DAgger with MSE on the teacher's actions, depth latency simulated in phase 2, actor initialized from the teacher, and low-res depth (48×64 to 58×87). Our 212×120 frames are about 5× more pixels than 58×87, so downsampling (for example to about 60×106) is consistent with this literature. That size is my suggestion, not a cited value.
- Our dap teacher should see the partner palm state *with the same 1-frame delay* the student sees (our choice; Agarwal's "Noisy" elevation-map baseline simulates 40 ms latency in both phases, their main scandot teacher states none). Otherwise the teacher's actions depend on information the student cannot have.
- Budget arithmetic: at about 95 depth steps/s/core × roughly 10 usable cores ≈ 950 steps/s, Agarwal's 6M-step phase 2 would take about 1.75 h and RMA's 80M about 23 h. At about 620 privileged steps/s/core × 10 cores ≈ 6,200 steps/s, 50M teacher steps take about 2.2 h. The dap task is far lower-dimensional than locomotion (one arm, kinematic, 20–100-step episodes), so teacher budgets of 10^7 rather than 10^9 are plausible. This is unverified.
- Every successful student in this set has memory (GRU/TCN/LSTM) or explicit history. A 3-frame stack (as in DrQ-v2) gives velocity but not acceleration over longer windows. A GRU on top of the stack is cheap insurance if partner speed changes mid-episode.

### Gaps
- Lee 2020: exact teacher and student sample counts and wall-clock were not found in the main-text extraction (probably in supplementary tables).
- LbC Table 1: the mapping of 64% and 96% to the white-box-only and on-policy-only variants could not be confirmed from the extracted text.
- Visual Dexterity: number of environments, sample counts and the exact GPU breakdown were not reported in the portion read.
- I found no paper that reports a teacher-student pipeline for a *moving-target contact/interception* arm task specifically.

## Q2. Asymmetric actor-critic (Pinto et al., RSS 2018) and related privileged-critic methods

### Takeaway
Giving the critic full simulator state while the actor sees only images is a nearly free change for actor-critic RL. Pinto et al. found it "outperforms symmetric inputs by significant margins" on Particle, Reacher and Fetch Pick, and it transferred to a real Fetch with domain randomization. Later work (OpenAI 2018/2019, Scaffolder 2024) generalizes this to privileged value functions, world models and rewards. For PPO fine-tuning of a depth student, an asymmetric critic is the standard choice.

### Cited Findings
- Method: "the critic is trained on full states while the actor (or policy) gets rendered images as input". Base optimizers were DDPG and HER. "Since the critic works on full state, it learns the state-action value function much faster, which also allows for better updates for the actor" — [arXiv 1710.06542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06542)
- Results: "asymmetric input versions of both DDPG and HER perform much better than their symmetric counterparts" (Particle). On Fetch Pick, "the asymmetric version of HER performs significantly better than the symmetric version". Overall, "asymmetric inputs for training outperforms symmetric inputs by significant margins". Results are shown as learning curves; I extracted no single headline number — [arXiv 1710.06542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06542)
- Observation and transfer: Fetch Pick uses "RGB images (100×100×3)". Abstract and method say RGBD. Domain randomization covered "texture, lighting, camera location and depth", with "uniform noise to the depth" and randomized camera orientation and focal length. Real-robot pick, push and move-block worked "without any training on the physical system". Policy bottlenecks further "improve the stability and speed of training" — [arXiv 1710.06542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06542)
- OpenAI 2018 used the same idea in PPO: the value network got privileged object and hand state that the policy lacked — [ar5iv 1808.00177](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.00177)
- Scaffolder (ICLR 2024) uses privileged sensing "in critics, world models, reward estimators, and other such auxiliary components that are only used at training time". It is evaluated on the "S3" suite of ten simulated robotic tasks — [arXiv 2405.14853](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.14853)
- Nguyen et al. (CoRL 2022): a SAC agent that "balances performing actions similar to the state expert and getting high returns under partial observability". It beat pure IL, pure RL, and sequential or parallel combinations on six robotics domains, and transferred to a physical robot "in a manipulation task from pixels" — [arXiv 2211.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01991)

### Inferences
- For dap: the PPO teacher is already symmetric on privileged state. For the optional PPO fine-tuning of the depth student, the critic should take privileged partner state (plus the student's own joint state) and the actor should take depth + joints. This is standard and adds no deployment cost.
- An asymmetric critic is not by itself a fix for the imitation gap. It improves value estimates, but the actor still has to infer the hidden state from observations.

### Gaps
- Pinto et al. give curves rather than tabulated success rates, so I extracted no scalar "asymmetric vs symmetric" number. Known theoretical caveats about asymmetric critics being biased in POMDPs (e.g., Baisero & Amato) were not checked in this pass.

## Q3. Pixel RL from scratch: DrQ, DrQ-v2, CURL, RAD — sample efficiency, frame stacks, augmentation

### Takeaway
Data augmentation (random shift/crop) is what makes model-free pixel RL work. DrQ-v2 solves most DeepMind Control tasks in about 1–3M frames and about 8 h on one GPU, from 3 stacked 84×84 frames. That is still roughly 10^6–10^7 environment steps per task, and our depth renderer is about 6.5× slower than privileged state. Distillation from a privileged teacher is the cheaper path, and Agarwal 2022 and Robot Parkour both report direct depth RL failing or being intractable.

### Cited Findings
- DrQ: a "simple data augmentation technique … to regularize the value function" on SAC, "without the need for auxiliary losses or pre-training". It surpassed Dreamer, PlaNet, SLAC and CURL on DMC — [arXiv 2004.13649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.13649)
- DrQ-v2 changes from DrQ: SAC→DDPG, n-step returns, "bilinear interpolation to the random shift image augmentation", an exploration noise schedule, and a larger replay buffer. Mini-batch 256 and learning rate 1e-4 "results into more stable training" — [arXiv 2107.09645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09645)
- DrQ-v2 observations: "stacks of 3 consecutive RGB images of size 84×84, stacked along the channel dimension to enable inference of dynamic information like velocity and acceleration". Action repeat is 2 — [arXiv 2107.09645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09645)
- DrQ-v2 compute: "majority of tasks taking just 8 hours to train on a single GPU". Throughput is "96" FPS, so "≈2.9 hours to train for 1M environment steps" on a V100. The 12-task benchmark plot runs to 3×10^6 frames; humanoid walk needs up to 30×10^6 frames. It matches DreamerV2 sample efficiency "4× faster" in wall-clock — [arXiv 2107.09645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.09645)
- CURL: contrastive auxiliary loss plus off-policy RL, with "1.9x and 1.2x performance gains at the 100K environment and interaction steps benchmarks" (DMC and Atari). Described as "the first image-based algorithm to nearly match the sample-efficiency of methods that use state-based features" — [arXiv 2004.04136](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.04136)
- RAD: studied augmentations on pixel and state inputs and introduced "random translate and random amplitude scale". Crop, translate, color jitter, cutout, random convolutions and amplitude scale "enable simple RL algorithms to outperform complex state-of-the-art methods" on DMC — [arXiv 2004.14990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14990)
- Direct depth RL in robotics: Agarwal says direct RL on depth "would require billions of samples" because rendering is about 10× slower — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638). Robot Parkour's "No Distill" PPO-from-depth baseline "cannot learn climbing, leaping and tilting" (0% success on climb, leap and tilt) — [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)
- Depth-specific augmentation used in sim-to-real robotics: pixel Gaussian noise, depth clipping and random artifacts (Parkour, [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)); uniform depth noise and camera pose/FOV randomization (Pinto, [arXiv 1710.06542](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06542)); point-cloud noise (Visual Dexterity, [ar5iv 2211.11744](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.11744)); latency and update-rate randomization (Agarwal, [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638))

### Inferences
- Our 3-frame stack matches DrQ-v2's convention for recovering velocity, which is what partner palm velocity requires. Random-shift augmentation (DrQ-style) carries over directly to depth images and is cheap. Applying it identically across the stacked frames keeps the motion cues intact (DrQ practice; not separately cited here).
- Throughput: at about 95 depth steps/s/core, a DrQ-v2-scale 1–3M-step from-scratch run takes about 3–9 h of pure simulation on one core, or about 0.3–0.9 h on 10 parallel cores, before network-update cost. Feasible as a baseline, but DMC "medium" tasks are not obviously comparable to a sparse antiparallel-contact objective. Teacher → DAgger is the safer main path, with pixel-RL-from-scratch as a comparison arm. This is a planning inference, not a cited result.
- Off-policy DrQ-v2 is far more sample-efficient than on-policy PPO for pixels. If we do pixel RL from scratch on a laptop, an off-policy augmented learner is better supported by the literature than PPO.

### Gaps
- I did not extract DrQ's exact pad size (commonly ±4 pixels on 84×84) from the paper text, so the precise augmentation magnitude is unconfirmed here.
- I found no study of augmentations specifically for *depth* in DMC-style pixel RL. The depth augmentation evidence comes from sim-to-real robotics papers.

## Q4. The imitation gap: teacher-student failure modes and fixes

### Takeaway
When the teacher acts on information the student cannot infer, even in principle from its history, DAgger/BC converges to the teacher's policy averaged over that hidden information. Weihs et al. call this the "imitation gap", and it can be arbitrarily bad, for example in "PoisonedDoors". The fixes are of three kinds. One mixes IL and RL losses adaptively (ADVISOR, TGRL, Nguyen et al.). One adapts the teacher to what the student can see (A2D). One designs the teacher's privileged inputs to be inferable (Agarwal's FOV/scandot choice, RMA's low-dim latents, Lee's history-based students).

### Cited Findings
- Definition: "when the teaching agent makes decisions with access to privileged information that is unavailable to the student, this information is marginalized during imitation learning, resulting in an 'imitation gap'". "the imitation gap's size directly impacts agent performance". IL-then-RL "gradual progression fails for tasks that require frequent switches between exploration and memorization" — [arXiv 2007.12173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- ADVISOR: an auxiliary actor trained by pure IL tells, per state, how well the student *can* imitate. The IL weight is "large when the auxiliary actor can reproduce the teacher's action with high confidence", and RL takes over elsewhere. Tested on 13 tasks (gridworlds, MiniGrid, multi-agent particle CoopNav, AI2-THOR/Habitat PointNav and ObjectNav) with PPO and MADDPG. It "outperforms pure imitation, pure reinforcement learning, as well as their sequential and parallel combinations" — [arXiv 2007.12173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.12173)
- A2D (Warrington et al., ICML 2021): "the expert does not know what the trainee cannot see, and so may encourage actions that are sub-optimal, even unsafe". The fix trains the expert to "maximize the expected reward of the imitating agent policy" (adaptive asymmetric DAgger) — [arXiv 2012.15566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.15566)
- TGRL (ICML 2023): balances RL and teacher-student objectives by "comparing the agent's performance to the counterfactual scenario of the agent learning without teacher supervision". Teacher weight rises if supervision helps and falls otherwise, which removes the hand-tuned IL/RL coefficient — [arXiv 2307.03186](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03186)
- Impossibly Good Experts (Walsman et al., ICLR 2023): experts with more information are "Impossibly Good" because "no learning algorithm will be able to reproduce their behavior". The paper gives necessary criteria under which advice alone recovers the best reduced-information policy, and "Elf Distillation" for the case where rewards must be used. Evaluated on MiniGrid and VizDoom — [ICLR 2023](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/poster/11786), [MLAnthology](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2023/walsman2023iclr-impossibly)
- Nguyen et al. (CoRL 2022) make the same trade-off inside SAC — [arXiv 2211.01991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.01991)
- Robotics practice that keeps the gap small by design:
  - Agarwal chose "scandots and camera field-of-view such that phase 2 loss is low", and Thm 2.1 bounds student return loss by the action gap η — [arXiv 2211.07638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07638)
  - RMA compresses privileged info to an 8-D latent regressed from 0.5 s of history, which loses only 76.2→73.5% success — [ar5iv 2107.04034](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.04034)
  - Extreme Parkour made the oracle heading an explicit prediction target, because the student cannot be told the heading at deployment — [arXiv 2309.14341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.14341)
  - In Robot Parkour, the gap between oracle and distilled student is visible: tilt 100→73%, climb 95→86% — [arXiv 2309.05665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05665)
- The value of memory for inferring hidden state: Lee 2020 TCN-100 vs TCN-1 (35.5% lower deviation) — [arXiv 2010.11251](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.11251); OpenAI LSTM vs FF policy (median 13 vs 3.5 successes) — [ar5iv 1808.00177](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1808.00177)

### Inferences
- What the dap student can recover: partner palm pose (in camera view) and velocity, from the 3-frame stack with 1-frame latency. What it cannot recover: any *future* information, such as the sampled partner speed profile, a future speed change, or the partner's goal pose if the teacher sees it. It also cannot recover palm state while the palm is occluded or out of the camera's FOV, or exact palm orientation if the depth resolution cannot resolve it.
- Recommendation: give the teacher only the *current, delayed* palm pose and velocity, not partner trajectory parameters. This keeps the gap close to zero, in the spirit of Agarwal's "choose privileged inputs so phase 2 loss is low". If richer privileged info is used, budget for an IL+RL combination (TGRL or ADVISOR-style weighting, or DAgger followed by PPO with an asymmetric critic).
- Diagnostic: track the DAgger action-MSE floor per state type. Where it stays high (e.g., partner just entering the view, or near-contact occlusion by the robot's own arm), the student lacks the information. That is the signal to add memory, change the camera, or drop that privileged input.

### Gaps
- No quantitative real-robot evidence was found comparing ADVISOR, TGRL or A2D in continuous-control manipulation from depth. Their benchmarks are mostly gridworld, navigation or toy POMDPs.

## Q5. Manipulation-specific depth/point-cloud policies trained in sim (handovers, dexterous grasping): methods and compute budgets

### Takeaway
The manipulation papers closest to dap — vision-based human-to-robot handover (Christen 2023, GenH2R 2024) and dexterous grasping or reorientation (DexPoint 2022, Visual Dexterity 2023) — all use point clouds from depth rather than raw depth images, and a privileged expert (a planner or an RL teacher). Christen's is the closest analogue of our task: pretraining from a privileged planner, then RL fine-tuning with a BC regularizer toward the expert. Reported compute is a single V100-class GPU for tens of hours (Christen about 72–96 h; Visual Dexterity "< 400 h").

### Cited Findings
- Christen et al. (CVPR 2023, handovers from point clouds): the expert is the OMG motion and grasp planner using ground-truth state. The student sees an egocentric wrist RGB-D point cloud (hand and object segmented), encoded by PointNet++, and outputs 6-DoF end-effector deltas — [ar5iv 2303.17592](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2303.17592)
  - Training: pretraining mixes planner demos with RL exploration in a replay buffer (with occasional DAgger-style expert actions). Fine-tuning uses TD3 with actor loss λ·L_BC (toward a frozen pretrained expert) + (1−λ)·L_DDPG + an auxiliary grasp-pose loss.
  - Budget: 10,000 + 5,000 iterations, about 72–96 h on one V100.
  - HandoverSim s0 success: sequential 75.23% (vs GA-DDPG 50.00%); simultaneous 68.75% (vs 36.81%). Fine-tuning improved over pretraining only from 73.38→75.23% (sequential) and 62.27→68.75% (simultaneous). Real pilot study: 41/60 vs GA-DDPG 21/60 and 33/60.
- Same paper: "interacting with humans remains challenging due to the difficulties of simulating humans". The framework is "trained with a human-in-the-loop via a two-stage teacher-student framework that uses motion and grasp planning, reinforcement learning, and self-supervision" — [arXiv 2303.17592](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17592)
- GenH2R (CVPR 2024): a procedural handover simulator with scene count "surpassing … existing simulators by three orders of magnitude", "a million high-quality demonstrations" from a "distillation-friendly demonstration generation method", and "a 4D imitation learning method augmented by a future forecasting objective" to distill into a visuomotor policy — [arXiv 2401.00929](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.00929)
- DexPoint (CoRL 2022): point-cloud RL with "imagined hand point clouds as augmented inputs" and "contact-based rewards", with an Allegro hand grasping novel objects in sim and real — [arXiv 2211.09423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09423)
- Visual Dexterity: an "imagined"/proprioceptive robot point cloud from forward kinematics is merged with the camera cloud. This is the same idea as DexPoint and a cheap way to give the student self-geometry — [ar5iv 2211.11744](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.11744)
- Compute range across the set (cited above): OpenAI 2018 used 384×16 CPU cores + 8 V100s (about 50 h for full randomization); Visual Dexterity < 400 h on one V100; Christen about 72–96 h on one V100; Robot Parkour 12+6 h per skill on one RTX 3090 plus 4×3090 for distillation; Extreme Parkour < 20 h on one 3090; Agarwal 13 h + 6 h on one 3090; RMA about 24 h + 3 h on one GPU desktop.

### Inferences
- Christen et al. is the best-matched precedent for dap's "meet a moving human hand" framing. Their recipe (privileged planner/teacher → student pretraining → RL fine-tuning with a BC regularizer toward the frozen teacher) is very close to our plan (PPO teacher → DAgger → PPO fine-tune). It is a citable justification for keeping a BC/KL term during the fine-tuning phase.
- All of these compute budgets assume GPU-parallel simulation (Isaac Gym) or GPU training. Our Drake + CPU laptop setup is orders of magnitude below Agarwal's 2e5 steps/s. Choosing a low-dimensional task (kinematic, short episodes), small depth resolution and a compact CNN (Parkour-sized: 3 conv layers of 16/32/32 channels + GRU 256) is what makes the plan feasible. This is an inference from the throughput numbers above.
- A point-cloud student (PointNet-style) is the manipulation-community norm. A 2D depth CNN is the locomotion norm and is cheaper. With a single fixed head camera and one target (the partner palm), a 2D depth CNN is a defensible simplification. No source directly compares the two for this kind of task.

### Gaps
- GenH2R's training compute and network details were not extracted (abstract only).
- DexPoint's sample counts and compute were not extracted (abstract only).
- No source found reports a Drake/CPU-only teacher-student depth pipeline with throughput numbers, so the laptop budget cannot be benchmarked against a published equivalent.
